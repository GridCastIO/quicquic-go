[B12 (#1385)](https://github.com/the-sarge/quic-go-fast/issues/1385) publishes the positive [B11 comparison (#1384)](2026-11-15-bbr-b11/README.md) under [continuation #1374](https://github.com/the-sarge/quic-go-fast/issues/1374) and the [accepted B12 contract](../adr/2026-10-24-multipath-bbr-plan.md#b12--publish-aggregation-outcome-and-retire-experiment-activation) at `76954048981e4c0da3f1618ef3038fe1ca7d5576`. This supersedes earlier public aggregation-refusal statements; frozen historical records retain their meaning.

## Public profile and limitations

`Config.ConfigureMultipathV1(true, 2, "aggregate")` selects the existing paced byte-round-robin scheduler with explicitly selected `bbrv3`. The supported profile remains experimental opt-in draft-21 QUIC v1 on Linux amd64, one or two paths and exact granted native UDP sockets. Either setter order is supported; the common `Transport.admitMultipath` gate checks the final controller/policy pair for all constructors and server-selected configurations. Changing a cloned aggregate config to Reno cannot bypass refusal. Reno stays the default, Reno aggregate remains rejected, and Reno/BBR primary/backup remains supported. Disabled or unnegotiated connections stay ordinary.

This is uncoupled multipath. B11's 36 valid observations used 37 charged attempts, retaining one contaminated observation and its single replacement. Its unchanged median round-paired gates were 1.9904570227887481 versus the better single path, 0.9910184032176794 versus the independent pair, and 1.0443982142403438 for the shared-bottleneck Reno competitor. The shared round-one ratio 0.8930542769 remains below 0.90 individually; the accepted median rule was not changed and no clean result was rerun. Unequal-path STREAM completion and DATAGRAM latency/delivery remain separate diagnostics in the B11 receipt. These fixed observations establish neither universal throughput nor per-round fairness, equal rates, statistical confidence, bounded application delay or an adoption recommendation. Pacing precedes byte ranking; the inherited “about one handoff” comment is not a numerical service-gap guarantee. BBR `ConnectionStats.PacketsLost` is not maintained, so its zeros are not proof of zero raw drops; retained relay observations own that evidence.

The [B9 support record (#1382)](2026-11-15-bbr-b9-support.md) preserves the exact accepted #1368 cost exceptions: mixed-bulk goodput 0.944295141271208 and CPU/useful-byte 1.2081314772756437; stateless-pressure CPU/useful-byte 1.1593702973325581 (goodput 0.9590708299335772). Raw failures and original thresholds remain unchanged. Underlying BBR is still unqualified; full [Q2 (#599)](https://github.com/the-sarge/quic-go-fast/issues/599) remains deferred. These exact-result exceptions cannot transfer to changed measured behavior.

## Applicability and retirement

B11 measured candidate `8c34fbac874adf55f096b6c67d51244160169422`; B12 starts at `0e1cf6ab028597825f57db67c09941431491943e`. Production differences in B12 are confined to public config selection, the common admission predicate and copying the configured scheduler at activation. The value selected for BBR aggregate is the identical B10 `scheduleAggregate`; scheduler ranking/ledger, readiness planning, BBR algorithms/sampling, recovery, native construction/completion/lifetime, wire handling and timers are unchanged. B11 therefore applies to the promoted runtime; source applicability is not another performance measurement. Ordinary connections return before the new admission predicate, and primary/backup retains the zero scheduler value. B9/B10's ordinary, native, Reno and primary/backup applicability carries with every original limitation.

The unexported experiment selector is removed. Public config is the only production activation authority, captured once by the connection-owned registry. `aggregation_test.go`, `interop/multipath/aggregation.py` and its finite accounting tests are retired after the decision; their measured revisions remain in Git history. All B11 observations, identity hashes, logs, ledgers and independent arithmetic/source audits retain their original bytes. Archived evidence is metadata and has no executable dependency on the retired runner or old overlay.

## Finite validation

Configuration tests cover selection, failed-call nonmutation, disabled state, cloning and removal of private activation. Constructor tests cover both setter orders, regular/early Dial and Listen, server-selected configs and final-controller rejection. Public aggregate transfer exercises both paths, DATAGRAMs and endpoint joins; the existing negative-profile, ordinary fallback and BBR feedback/lifecycle suites remain preservation evidence. These maintained test aids retain transport-suite ownership and equivalent-replacement/feature-removal retirement.

The existing independent-peer fixture accepts an explicit policy parameter while keeping default Reno primary/backup and its 16-case inventory. The six BBR aggregate cases are useful-transfer, abandon-reopen-late-feedback and same-id-migration-rebinding in both client/server roles. Useful-transfer preserves per-path STREAM/DATAGRAM checks and adds a bounded phase that activates both live paths through `SetLocalUseV1`, snapshots sent STREAM counters after activation, offers bounded useful work, and requires new sent STREAM traffic on both while they remain active within the existing 30-second context. Lifecycle cases retain deliberate per-path preference. Native case identities, actual charges and final review/certification evidence are retained with the product PR; the support claim requires their completion before merge.

All six cases passed without skips or replacements at source `73b0d6707ae215a9f49033efb300ff1d1a37eaa4`, tree `124195e99b8af2dc9ca0741b5fc970c2707f18ee`, using native Minimax Linux amd64 kernel `7.0.0-30-generic`, Go 1.27.4 and the unchanged pinned peer/container toolchain. The [matrix receipt](2026-11-15-bbr-b12/native/results/matrix-result.json), [raw logs](2026-11-15-bbr-b12/native/results/) and outer charge receipts retain the actual image/binary/driver identities and both-active STREAM witnesses. Fixture CPUs 4–7 and SMT siblings 20–23 were inspected; unrelated native host jobs remained unchanged. These short functional examples make no timing-performance claim. Setup, six case envelopes and validation charge **6 instances /25.34940649755298942 host-seconds** once, without adding inner durations. Subsequent differences are whitespace-only test formatting and documentation/evidence; runtime and executed peer/driver source remain identical. This is source applicability, not binary identity for a later head. Native root/ACK-handler suites, one focused lifecycle race and the Darwin ordinary full suite passed. Final exact-head certification and hosted Reno evidence belong to PR #1393's durable discussion.
